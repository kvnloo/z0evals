## 1. The owner's goals (z0intelligence README + ROADMAP, paraphrased)

- z0intelligence is a provenance-backed cognition and control plane for agent systems (Hermes, Claude Code, OMP, ...).
  Success = **verified outcomes stay equal or improve while expensive generic (frontier) inference decreases**: fewer
  frontier calls, less irrelevant context, fewer repetitive decisions, lower latency.
- Mechanism-neutral promotion: rule, cache, ridge/linear, mushroom-body (MB) "fly" learner, logit-readout head
  (Jev/NanoJev), small SLM, or anything else wins only by beating simple baselines on a sealed holdout, with risk/coverage,
  calibration (Brier/log loss), fallback, invalidators and shadow mode before canary.
- First target decision families (ROADMAP P3/P5): intent/skill routing, tool-family selection, context relevance,
  retry/recover/escalate, verification-needed/done, model/effort routing. "Army of flies" = many independent bounded
  specialists, not one universal model. "Shadow network" idea = several SLMs running beside Jev as decision models,
  improved by meta methods (Bradley-Terry, autoresearch, SFT, RSI/GEPA curricula).
- Hard non-goals: replacing frontier models on open-ended reasoning; letting the training loop modify its own judge or
  sealed eval; uploading the raw private corpus anywhere public.

## 2. The owner's approach idea (what he asked for today)

1. Reuse **Southpaw's evolutionary-training repo** (kvnloo fork of SouthpawIN/evolutionary-training). Its point is that
   the training process is scaffolded into **Hermes profiles**: a `gym-trainer` profile runs four loops (A: CMA-ES
   "Darwin" weight-space merges of parent models -> benchmark -> keep best; B: SFT/GRPO on agentic/tool-calling data ->
   eval; C: data ingestion; D: reporting), and a `gym-evaluator` profile independently assesses releases. Source
   headline claim (not reproduced): Darwin-27B-Opus 86.9% GPQA Diamond with merges only.
2. Use those profiles to run **the process from the blog "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming"** (rikiyat.github.io):
   a frontier LLM reads raw weights in a sandbox and writes weight edits directly, no gradients.
3. "Go meta": train/edit tiny SLMs (<1B) **at work that helps z0intelligence's goals**, not at math.
4. Work the size ladder **from both ends in parallel**: smallest (SmolLM2-135M, Gemma-3-270M) and largest under 1B
   (Qwen3.5-0.8B, Qwen3-0.6B).

2. IS FRONTIER WEIGHT EDITING THE RIGHT LEVER?

Not as the primary lever for z0 decisions. (I)

The evidence says weight editing removes a named generative failure mode; it does not add capability:

- invalid-format / no-answer output (SmolLM2-135M instruct: 99% no-answer on MATH-500, (M));

- systematic overconfidence;
- looping.

Falsifier: under the same forward-pass budget and the same tuning data, the editor's best edit fails to beat, by a bootstrap CI on held-out items, the best of three controls:
- (i) a random rank-1 projection with the same norm and layers;
- (ii) a logit bias plus temperature fit on the tuning set (about 10 parameters);
- (iii) LoRA rank 4 on the same items.

1. Amdahl diagnosis

Inference: but next_action top-1 is the wrong objective for "frontier does less work". Predicting the family the frontier will pick does not remove the frontier call unless the prediction is good enough to skip the call — and 0.41 is not. The measured volume that actually changed frontier work is observation-pack shortening (26.3% of frontier-bound units, all from one mechanism). The decision family with the best ratio of (frontier tokens saved) x (labels available) is therefore context relevance / tool-output summarization gating, and secondarily verification-needed. The Hermes step log gives free labels for "was this tool output ever referenced later in the trajectory" (a derived, redactable boolean per observation), at n in the tens of thousands.

Inference, first-target recommendation: a sub-1B scorer for "will this observation be used downstream" (keep / shorten / drop) with calibrated abstention, trained on derived features from the vault. Tool-family next_action is the second target, as a rule-vs-ridge-vs-SLM bake-off, not an SLM-first project.

2. Is frontier weight editing the right lever?

Source: Exp 8's 48.1/67.1/62.3 came with %cut going 93.0 -> 33.1; the edits were a rank-1 projection removal or a zeroed head. Gemma-3-270M, which rarely loops, did not move more than ~1 point. Our Measured read/write replication (7/7 reads; Astra Breakout 586.4) confirms the frontier can read and write small weight sets, but the Exp-8 pilot has produced zero local results (OOM) and is only now deployed on Modal.

Inference: weight editing is a failure-mode surgery tool, not a training method. It plausibly wins only when (i) the target has one dominant, nameable, measurable failure mode, (ii) that mode is linear-ish and localized (a direction or a head), and (iii) removing it uncovers latent capability that already exists. For z0 decision tasks the candidate modes are: invalid-format output (SmolLM2 99% no-answer on MATH-500 is exactly this), majority-class collapse on a 7-way label, and overconfidence on abstain. Of these, invalid-format is the Gemma/Qwen analogue and the most likely to be editable.

Ranking for our tasks: (b) frozen SLM + trained logit/linear head (Jev-style) first — it is what NanoJev already does at 31 ms and it cannot collapse formats; (d) ridge/MB as the control that must be beaten; (a) LoRA-SFT when the head plateaus; (c) Darwin merges only as a cheap way to pick a better frozen trunk for (b); weight editing last, as a one-shot repair applied to a trunk that has a measured named failure.
